# PrepRally — Market Research

**Date:** August 11, 2026

## 1. Market size and demand

- The global test-preparation market is worth **$50B+** and growing; Technavio projects it to grow by **$16.28B between 2024–2028**, driven by online prep and AI ([Technavio via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/test-preparation-market-to-grow-by-usd-16-28-billion-from-2024-2028--driven-by-online-test-prep-emphasis-with-ai-driving-market-transformation---technavio-302371306.html), [Market Reports World](https://www.marketreportsworld.com/market-reports/test-preparation-market-14722349)).
- **~1.9–2M US students take the SAT each year**; many colleges have re-instated test requirements since 2024, pushing SAT volume back up.
- Gamification is the specific tailwind for this product: gamified test-prep attracts **~34% of learners** and shows **~40% higher retention** than traditional materials, with **22% better engagement/retention** overall ([WifiTalents test-prep statistics](https://wifitalents.com/test-prep-industry-statistics/)).
- 52% of learners already use online test-prep platforms — the behavior shift to digital is done; the unclaimed space is *social* digital prep.

## 2. Competitive landscape

| Product | What it is | SAT-authentic content | Live multiplayer | Free | Gap PrepRally exploits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bluebook** (College Board) | Official testing app + practice tests | ✅ The real thing | ❌ | ✅ | Zero social features, zero fun |
| **OnePrep** | 4,000+ free DSAT questions, Bluebook-clone UI, filters by domain/difficulty ([oneprep.com](https://www.oneprep.com/question-bank)) | ✅ Very high | ❌ Solo only | ✅ | The strongest content competitor — but entirely single-player |
| **Khan Academy** | Official free SAT practice partner | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ | Solo, no game feel |
| **Kahoot** | Live quiz game, join-by-code | ❌ user-made trivia | ✅ | Freemium | Trivia-length questions; can't render passages/math like the real test |
| **Blooket / Gimkit / Quizizz / Wayground** | Kahoot-style classroom games ([comparison](https://rigorousthemes.com/blog/best-games-like-kahoot-quizizz-free-included/), [Jotform roundup](https://www.jotform.com/blog/kahoot-alternatives/)) | ❌ | ✅ | Freemium | Same: engines built for trivia, not SAT-format items |
| **UWorld / Princeton Review / Kaplan** | Paid prep courses & QBanks | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ ($$$) | Expensive, solo, adult-feeling |

**The gap:** every product is strong on exactly one axis. Authentic-content products (Bluebook, OnePrep, Khan) have no multiplayer; multiplayer products (Kahoot family) have no authentic content. PrepRally sits in the empty quadrant: **authentic Digital SAT questions × live social play**.

Validation that the demand exists: people already run "SAT Prep Game Night" sessions on Schoolhouse.world using generic tools ([schoolhouse.world](https://schoolhouse.world/series/19482)), and teachers routinely stuff SAT questions into Kahoot despite the format not fitting ([Progress Learning on SAT prep games](https://progresslearning.com/news-blog/free-online-sat-prep-games/)).

## 3. The Digital SAT format we must match

Per the current (2026) Digital SAT structure ([EdisonOS breakdown](https://www.edisonos.com/digital-sat/structure-breakdown), [TestPrepKart guide](https://www.testprepkart.com/sat/blog/digital-sat-exam-pattern-a-comprehensive-guide)):
- Two sections, 98 questions, 2h14m, adaptive two-module design, 400–1600 scale, no penalty for guessing.
- **Reading & Writing:** 54 questions; every question has its own short passage. Domains: Information & Ideas, Craft & Structure, Expression of Ideas, Standard English Conventions.
- **Math:** 44 questions; calculator allowed throughout; ~75% multiple choice, ~25% student-produced response. Domains: Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving & Data Analysis, Geometry & Trigonometry.

Product implication: PrepRally's tagging taxonomy and question rendering copy this structure exactly, so practice transfers to test day (OnePrep's Bluebook-pixel-match approach proves students value this).

## 4. Go-to-market (realistic for a student founder)

1. **Friends-first (weeks 1–4):** launch at your own school; the join code *is* the invite. Track players-per-rally.
2. **Clubs & classes (months 2–3):** pitch teachers/tutors a free class activity; one teacher = 25 players instantly.
3. **Communities (months 2–6):** r/Sat (600k+ members), Discord SAT servers, TikTok study-tok clips of live rallies (Kahoot's virality was screen-recorded gameplay).
4. **SEO later:** free question bank pages, like OnePrep did.

## 5. Pricing research

- Students expect free. OnePrep, Khan, Bluebook are free — content alone cannot be paywalled.
- What students/parents do pay for: analytics, score prediction, full banks, convenience ($10–40/mo range for UWorld/LearnQ-type products; PrepRally should undercut hard at **$4–5/mo** if/when it charges).
- Kahoot's proven model: individuals free forever, organizations pay. Long-term revenue is tutors/schools, not teenagers.

## 6. Verdict

**Good idea, real gap, right founder-market fit** (a student building for their own friend group is exactly how Facebook/Discord-type loops start). Biggest risks are content volume (need hundreds of quality original questions — real SAT questions are copyrighted and cannot be copied) and the empty-room problem (mitigate with solo mode + async challenges). Ship the free web MVP now, measure players-per-rally and second-game retention, and only think about pricing after those look healthy.
